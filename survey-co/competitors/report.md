# Bay Area land surveyor scan

Research date: November 10, 2026. Territory: San Francisco, San Mateo, Santa Clara (south limit Gilroy), Alameda, Contra Costa, Marin, Sonoma, Napa, and Solano. Nothing in this file is a quote for a job. Blank cells in the spreadsheet mean the fact was not observed.

Firm inventory: `bay_area_competitors.csv` (45 firms). Screenshots: `screenshots/`.

## How this was gathered

Company pages were opened and read. Desktop screenshots were saved for the clearest and the weakest sites. Google search for "land surveyor san francisco" was blocked by Google's unusual-traffic page (`screenshots/google-captcha-block.png`), so Google Ads and Local Services Ads were not observed. The Meta Ad Library was opened. A direct Yelp search in this browser was blocked (`screenshots/yelp-sf.png`). Yelp ratings below come from search retrievals of Yelp URLs the same day, not from a live Yelp session. Google star counts, where filled, come from findlandsurveyor.com county pages whose own review date is September 2, 2026, unless a firm's site pasted review text. Those are not live Google lookups.

Firms south of Gilroy, and firms whose only office found was outside the nine counties, were left out. A Colorado firm named True North was not merged with a San Francisco directory name that happens to match.

## Key takeaways

1. The residential web experience is thin. Of the sites actually opened, the useful pattern is a phone number, a generic contact box, and a service list. Almost none ask for an APN, the county, the purpose, or site access before offering a number.
2. Published prices are rare. The only Bay Area dollar table found on a competitor site is Contour Survey's own blog. It does not show a dataset. Older directory averages (ProMatcher, dated in the source snippet to June 5, 2018) are far below 2025-2026 homeowner reports. Those reports are single jobs, not a market study.
3. A Record of Survey is the price surprise. One Oakland homeowner, posting August 27, 2026, was quoted $4,200 for the boundary work and about $4,583 more for a recorded map and county fees. Firms rarely explain that split before the quote.
4. The firms that market well are still small. Contour, Transit, Action, NorthStar, and Bay Area Land Surveying explain services in plain language and put a quote button up front. The large civil firms (BKF, Sandis, Kier + Wright, MacKay & Somps, Towill) look current and barely speak to a homeowner.
5. Paid social and search ads were not found for these firms in the checks that loaded. Meta searches for "land surveyor," "Transit Land Surveying," and "Contour Survey" returned unrelated ads on the first screen. That is an observation about those screens, not proof that no surveyor buys ads.
6. Several long-running firms still ship unfinished or dated sites: a Wix placeholder on Westover, a keyword footer and hit counter at San Francisco Surveying Company, a 2011 copyright at Footsteps, and a "services are changing" banner at Hogan.
7. SB 9 lot splits and ADU site plans are already named by NorthStar, Hogan, Underwood & Rosenblum, Contour, Bay Area Land Surveying, and Footsteps. The intake behind those words is still a phone call or a one-box form.
8. License numbers are published by some small firms (Transit PLS 9817, Action PLS 9791, Westover PLS 7779, Contour PLS 9686, Phelps lists four numbers, Storrs L.S. 6914, Montclair 4961, Meridian PLS 6784) and omitted by most of the large sites.

## Price ranges, with sources

Do not blend these into one "typical" price. They measure different things.

### Bay Area, from a competitor's blog

Contour Survey, "How Much Does a Land Survey Cost in the Bay Area?", byline January 15, 2025, framed as a 2026 guide: https://www.contour-survey.com/blog/land-survey-cost-bay-area

The page says costs in San Francisco, Oakland, San Jose, and surrounding areas "typically range from $500 to $3,000+," and prints this table:

| Survey type on that page | Range printed |
| --- | --- |
| Boundary survey, residential lot | $600 - $1,500 |
| Boundary survey, larger parcels | $1,000 - $3,000+ |
| ALTA/NSPS | $2,000 - $5,000+ |
| Topographic | $1,000 - $3,500 |
| Construction staking | $500 - $2,000 |

The page does not say how many jobs or invoices sit behind the table. Treat it as that firm's planning guide. It names the same drivers the other sources name: lot size and shape, terrain and access, record research and missing monuments, survey type, and county filing and travel. It says San Francisco often costs more because of lot history and parking.

### Bay Area, single homeowner reports

These are anecdotes, not averages.

- r/BayAreaRealEstate, August 27, 2026, Oakland house of about 1,500-1,600 sq ft on a roughly 6,000 sq ft hillside lot. The poster said the only quote received was $4,200 for the boundary survey plus $4,000 for a Record of Survey and $583 in Alameda County review and filing fees. Another commenter said they spent $3,000 on a 5,500 sq ft lot in the South Bay on flat land. A third commenter said their own recent estimates were closer to $15,000 and that a lawyer had mentioned $25,000. Those last two figures are one person's report about unnamed firms. https://www.reddit.com/r/BayAreaRealEstate/comments/1w080jg/reasonable_cost_for_a_boundary_line_survey_on_sfh/
- r/BayAreaRealEstate, May 10, 2025. One commenter said a survey of a 3,800 sq ft lot cost $3,000. No firm named. https://www.reddit.com/r/BayAreaRealEstate/comments/1kh75wr/i_want_to_have_my_property_lines_marked_how_much/

The $583 county figure is what the homeowner said the surveyor included. It was not checked against an Alameda County fee schedule in this research.

### San Francisco and San Jose directory averages, older

ProMatcher cost pages, fetched November 10, 2026:

- San Francisco residential boundary survey up to 1/2 acre: $869.34, range $575.46 - $1,163.22. https://land-surveyors.promatcher.com/cost/san-francisco-ca-land-surveyors-costs-prices.aspx
- San Jose, same product: $912.66 was on the fetched page. A search snippet of that page also showed a range of $604.13 - $1,221.18 and the date June 5, 2018. The date was in the snippet, not in the portion of the page re-read on this pass. https://land-surveyors.promatcher.com/cost/san-jose-ca-land-surveyors-costs-prices.aspx

The pages say the figure excludes subdivision surveys, topographic surveying, water boundaries, difficult terrain, and jobs that need extensive research. They are not 2026 Bay Area quotes.

### California or national figures, not Bay Area invoices

- findlandsurveyor.com's detailed California cost guide, updated September 2, 2026, was fetched and deliberately does not print a dollar table. It says a fence, an ADU, a dispute, a commercial title survey, and a flood or coastal permit are different assignments, and that a proposal should state whether a Record of Survey or Corner Record is expected and whether county charges are included. https://findlandsurveyor.com/guides/land-survey-cost-california/
- A search snippet of https://findlandsurveyor.com/california/ (direct refetch returned 403) showed an Alameda County "typical boundary survey" of $800 to $2,500 and a statewide FAQ of $500 to $2,000 for most residential boundary surveys, $200 to $700 for elevation certificates, and ALTA/NSPS "typically start at $2,000." Those numbers were not re-read on the page. Label them as a directory estimate, not a Bay Area invoice study.
- Same directory's county explainers, review date September 2, 2026, give timing rather than price. San Francisco: a standard residential boundary "typically takes two to four weeks." Sonoma cities such as Santa Rosa or Petaluma: two to four weeks, longer for rural, vineyard, and post-fire work. Marin and Solano explainers: field work often one to two days, office work another one to three weeks, and three to six weeks end to end for many projects. These are the directory's statements, not a firm's promise.

No firm page reviewed here published a turnaround in days for a finished residential boundary or topo survey. Geometrix says proposals are usually same-day. Contour says it responds within 24 hours and prices after a scope meeting.

## Website experience

### What the better sites do

Screenshots: `best-contour-survey.png`, `best-transit-land-surveying.png`, `best-action-land-surveyors.png`, `best-bay-area-land-surveying.png`, `best-northstar-survey.png`, `best-underwood-rosenblum.png`.

- Contour is the only site that combines a modern layout, a service menu a designer would recognize (ADU, parcel maps, ALTA, scanning, monument preservation), city pages, a blog, a named PLS number, and a quote path. The path is a scope meeting, and the copy says serious projects only.
- Transit puts "Get Free Estimate" on the hero, names the licensed surveyor, and shows testimonials that say the city and the job (fence, trees, rush survey). The contact form accepts a project address and files.
- Action puts "California Licensed PLS 9791" in the header and a Request A Quote button on every view of the header. The hero artwork in the screenshot was mostly empty.
- Bay Area Land Surveying's menu is the homeowner list: ADU, lot line adjustment, parcel map, condo plan, boundary.
- NorthStar's Google Site names ADU, SB 9, FEMA, ALTA, boundary, and topo on one screen and pastes Google review text. It does not show an average or a price.
- Underwood & Rosenblum's surveying page is clean, names SB 9, and then asks only for name, email, and message.

### What the weaker sites do

Screenshots: `worst-sf-surveying-company.png`, `worst-footsteps-surveying.png`, `worst-american-land-surveying.png`, `worst-cross-land-surveying.png`, `worst-westover-surveying.png`, `worst-golden-gate-surveying.png`.

- San Francisco Surveying Company is a single column with a historical photo, a visible keyword paragraph, and a hit counter. The email link is the call to action.
- Footsteps (Santa Clara) carries a 2011 copyright, a fixed desktop layout, a Gmail address, a Yelp text link, and a keyword footer. The quote is "contact us," not a form.
- American Land Surveying is a service list and two phone numbers. The about page had no viewport meta tag. Email is the estimate path.
- Cross is readable and specific (records of survey, corner records, the counties it actually works) and has nowhere to start except a contact form and a phone number.
- Westover's hero looks finished. The fetched HTML still contains Wix placeholder copy in the FEMA section.
- Golden Gate Surveying has a quote button and a nine-county page. The hero headline breaks mid-phrase. The page is a Google Site with little on the first screen beyond the button.

Common gaps:

- No price, and no sentence that says the first number will change if monuments are missing or a Record of Survey is required.
- Quote forms, when they exist, ask for a name and a message. Storrs and Montclair add a phone and a project description. Transit adds an address and a file upload. Nobody observed asked for APN, county, purpose, lot size, dogs, gates, or a deadline in a structured way.
- Testimonials are either absent or pasted without a link to the source review.
- Blogs and city pages are unusual. Contour has them. Geometrix and a few others explain one concept (record of survey versus corner record, what to send). Most sites stop at a list.
- Large firms hide the survey offer inside a civil menu and route everyone through a generic contact form.
- Mobile layout was not tested on a phone. Viewport meta tags were present on Action, Foresight, Storrs, Montclair, Contour's quote page, Transit's contact page, Underwood & Rosenblum, Hogan, Phelps, Bay Area Land Surveying, Kier + Wright, Sandis, Towill, BKF, and Adobe. American Land Surveying's about page did not have one.

## What to emulate and do better

Drawn from the sites above, not from ad-library impressions.

1. Contour's information architecture. One page per job a buyer already has a name for: boundary, topo, ALTA, elevation certificate, ADU, lot split, construction staking. Say who it is for in the first sentence. Then do what Contour does not: let a homeowner with a fence or an ADU submit the property without booking a "serious projects only" meeting.
2. Transit's proof. Put short quotes next to a city and a purpose. Ask for the address and the file on the first form, the way Transit does, and also ask the questions Transit leaves off (APN, county, purpose, access).
3. Action and Phelps on identity. A visible license number and a one-line description of who signs the map. Phelps goes further and says work starts after a signed scope. Copy that sequence. Do not copy Action's empty hero.
4. NorthStar and Bay Area Land Surveying on the menu. SB 9, ADU, FEMA, lot line adjustment, and parcel map should be words on the homepage, not only inside a PDF.
5. Geometrix on the pre-quote packet. Say, in plain language, that a boundary job usually needs the deed or a title report, and that the filing may be a record of survey or a corner record depending on what is found. Geometrix already says a version of this and promises same-day proposals. Publish the proposal speed. Do not publish a field-completion promise.
6. Underwood & Rosenblum's writing, not its form. One sentence per service is enough. Replace the name/email/message box with the intake in `../brief/surveying_brief.md`.
7. Cross's honesty about geography. Name the counties actually worked, including the Gilroy limit, instead of a keyword block of every city in Northern California.
8. A cost page that is labeled as a planning range and lists the drivers Contour already lists, plus the Record of Survey split the Oakland poster ran into. Link each range to the source or mark it as the firm's own guide. Do not present a single "Bay Area average."

## Positioning opportunities

1. The quote explains the filing before it states a fee. "Boundary marks" and "recorded map, if the law requires one" are separate lines. The August 2026 Oakland thread is the demand for that sentence.
2. A purpose picker for the jobs Bay Area clients actually book: fence, sale, permit / ADU / SB 9, lender or title, dispute, construction. NorthStar and Contour name some of these. None of the forms reviewed collect them as structured answers.
3. A real-estate-report path. Habu referrals can arrive with the address, APN, and a report already in hand. The intake should flag that source so the file does not start from a blank email. No competitor page reviewed offered a partner handoff.
4. Status without a phone tag. Contour shows a Portal link and almost no one else shows a client login. A page that says "records ordered / field scheduled / map in review / filing at the county" would be new relative to the sites opened.
5. ADU and SB 9 as a product, with the city named. Contour already says several cities want a current stamped site plan. A landing page per city, fed by the same intake, is open: the large firms do not do it, and the small firms that mention ADU still use a generic form.
6. Same-day "can we price this" rather than same-day survey. Geometrix and Contour already sell response speed. Match that, and say in the same sentence that field time and county review are not promised until the deed and the site are seen.
7. Show the deliverable. PDF, CAD, stakes, or a recorded map. Transit's reviews mention PDF and CAD. Most homepages never say what the client receives.
8. The unfinished-website gap is the brand gap. A current mobile page, a named signer, and a form that can be completed on a phone would be ahead of San Francisco Surveying Company, Footsteps, American, Westover's placeholder block, and the Google Site templates, without claiming to out-survey a 70-year firm on day one.
