For a landing page and a quote form. The company name is not chosen; this draft says [Survey Co]. Territory is the nine-county Bay Area, with Santa Clara County ending at Gilroy. Nothing south of Gilroy.

Research date: November 10, 2026. Prices are cited. Where a figure is not from the Bay Area, it is labeled. Where no figure was found, the cell is empty.

Section 5 is background for operations. It is not required on the company website for now. Do not publish a license number from this brief.

## 1. Services, in client language

Descriptions below follow how Bay Area firms and the public record talk about the product. They are not a scope of work and not a bid.

**Boundary survey / record of survey.** A licensed surveyor finds the property lines from deeds, maps, and monuments, and marks them. If what is found does not match the recorded map in the ways state law lists, the surveyor files a record of survey with the county. Some retracements can be documented with a corner record instead. The client should be told which document applies only after the records and the field work are reviewed.

**Corner record.** A shorter county filing used when a surveyor establishes or restores certain corners, or when a retracement finds enough original monumentation that a full record of survey is not required. It is not a substitute the client can choose in order to avoid a record of survey if the statute requires the longer map.

**ALTA/NSPS land title survey.** A survey for a commercial purchase or a loan. It combines the boundary with the items the title company and the lender ask for, often including easements, improvements, and selected Table A items. Contour's site says it prepares these to the 2026 ALTA/NSPS standard. Residential fence jobs are not this product.

**Topographic survey.** A map of the shape of the site and the visible features: contours, spot elevations, buildings, walls, and surface utilities as agreed. Architects and engineers use it for additions, grading, and permits. It is often combined with a boundary survey. A topo map by itself does not decide a property line.

**Construction staking.** Markers set from an approved plan so a contractor can build walls, foundations, curbs, or utilities where the plan shows them. Westover, Cross, Underwood & Rosenblum, Kier + Wright, and Sandis all list this. It is priced by the number of trips and points, which none of those sites publish.

**Lot line adjustment.** Moving a line between existing parcels without creating a new one, using the city's or county's process. Bay Area Land Surveying and Cross list it. Some jurisdictions ask for a lot line adjustment map; others ask for a parcel map waiver. The site should not promise which document a city will accept.

**Tentative map, parcel map, and subdivision map.** The maps used to divide land. A parcel map is the usual path for a smaller split. A tract or final map is the path for a larger one. Bay Area Land Surveying describes a parcel map as four or fewer parcels and a tract map as five or more. That is their wording; the local ordinance controls the actual threshold. Cinquini & Passarino and Foresight list subdivision and parcel maps.

**FEMA elevation certificate.** A signed form that reports how a building sits relative to the flood elevation on the flood map. Lenders and insurers ask for it. Some firms also help prepare a Letter of Map Amendment when the building is high enough to argue the flood zone should change. Meridian, Geometrix, Footsteps, NorthStar, and Contour list this. It is not a boundary survey.

**Easement plats and legal descriptions.** A drawing and a written description of an easement, a lot line, or another interest, for a deed or an agreement. The surveyor prepares the description. An attorney prepares the agreement. Geometrix tells condo clients they need an attorney for CC&Rs.

**As-built survey.** A map of what was built, as opposed to what the plan proposed. Used for closeout, condos, and ADA checks. American, Foresight, Contour, and Linda Carruthers list versions of this.

**SB 9 urban lot split.** A state lot-split path for certain residential parcels. NorthStar, Hogan, and Underwood & Rosenblum name SB 9. Whether a given lot qualifies is a planning question. The surveyor maps the split. The site should not say a parcel qualifies.

**ADU / permit site plan.** A current boundary and topo, or a site plan, that a city asks for before it will take an ADU or addition application. Contour says San Francisco, Oakland, Berkeley, and several Peninsula and Marin cities ask for a survey signed by a licensed surveyor for many of these permits. That is one firm's statement about practice, not a city-by-city code check. NorthStar, Hogan, Bay Area Land Surveying, and Footsteps also name ADU work.

**Other work seen on local sites.** Monument preservation before construction (Contour cites the duty to reference monuments that paving or grading would destroy). Settlement and deformation monitoring (Westover, Meridian, Foresight, Contour). Condo conversion plans (Geometrix, Bay Area Land Surveying, Storrs, Foresight). Story pole or setback checks (Foresight, Meridian). Expert-witness exhibits (Meridian, Geometrix, Linda Carruthers, Storrs). Hydrographic surveys and drone mapping show up at Meridian, Transit, Phelps, and Cinquini & Passarino. Those are real local offers. They are poor default products for a first residential form.

### What changes the price

From Contour's Bay Area blog, the findlandsurveyor.com California guide (September 2, 2026), and the Oakland filing example:

- Lot size and the number of corners. A long or irregular parcel takes longer than a small rectangle.
- Terrain, vegetation, and access. The blog calls out San Francisco hillsides, the Oakland Hills, and Marin. Gates, dogs, and a crew that cannot park are the practical version of "access." The form should ask.
- Record quality and missing monuments. Gold-rush deeds, a 1906-era block, a fire area where corners burned, or a neighbor dispute means more office time. Sonoma's directory page treats Tubbs (2017) and Kincade (2019) burn areas as a reason a survey takes longer.
- County filing. If a record of survey is required, the map, the county examination, and the recording fee are extra. The Oakland poster was shown that as a second line. The statute, not the client's preference, decides when the filing is mandatory. See section 5.
- Urgency and crew scheduling. Transit’s clients describe rush visits. No firm published a rush price.
- The deliverable. Stakes only, a PDF, CAD, an elevation certificate, or an ALTA Table A list are different jobs. Transit's reviews mention PDF and CAD. Contour's quote page asks the client to name the deliverable before the meeting.

## 3. Intake, in order

Required means the form should not submit a quote request without it. Nice-to-have can be skipped. Dropdown text is suggested, not a legal classification.

### Required to quote

1. Contact. Full name. Email. Phone. Role: Owner, Buyer, Owner's agent, Architect or designer, Contractor, Lender or title, Other.
2. Property address. Street, city, ZIP.
3. County. Dropdown: San Francisco, San Mateo, Santa Clara, Alameda, Contra Costa, Marin, Sonoma, Napa, Solano. If the ZIP is south of Gilroy or outside these counties, stop and say the firm does not cover it.
4. Purpose. One or more: Fence or wall, Sale or purchase, Refinance or lender, Title or escrow, Permit, ADU, SB 9 lot split, Other lot split or subdivision, Dispute with a neighbor, Construction staking, Elevation certificate or flood insurance, I am not sure.
5. Survey type if known. Optional only in the sense that "Not sure" is a valid choice, but the question itself should be required. Choices: Not sure, Boundary only, Boundary and topo, Topographic only, ALTA/NSPS, Elevation certificate, Lot line adjustment, Parcel or tentative map, SB 9 split, ADU or permit site plan, Construction staking, As-built, Easement or legal description.
6. Deadline. Date, plus a choice: No hard date, Before a contractor starts, City submittal, Escrow date, Other.
7. How they heard about [Survey Co]. Choices should include Habu referral as its own value, not buried in "other." Suggested list: Habu referral, Real estate agent, Architect or designer, Contractor, Previous client, Google, Yelp, Friend or neighbor, Other. Store Habu referral as a flag the CRM can filter.

### Nice to have

8. APN, and whose name is on the tax bill.
9. Lot size, as the client believes it. Choices: Under 5,000 sq ft, 5,000-10,000 sq ft, 10,000 sq ft to 1/2 acre, 1/2 acre to 2 acres, Over 2 acres, Not sure. Do not treat the answer as the surveyed area.
10. Terrain. Flat, Gentle slope, Steep, Wooded, Not sure.
11. Monuments. Choices: I can see survey markers, A prior surveyor left stakes, I have not looked, I think they are missing.
12. Prior survey or plat. Yes, I will upload it; Yes, but I do not have a copy; No; Not sure.
13. Title report or deed. Upload, or "title company will send it," or "I do not have one."
14. Site access. Gate code or lockbox, dogs, tenant who must be notified, crew can park on site, steep or overgrown areas the crew should know about. Free text plus checkboxes.
15. Document upload. Deed, prior map, title report, architect sketch, city comment letter. Cap the size; Transit's form caps files at 64 MB, which is a reasonable reference, not a requirement.
16. Anything the neighbor or the city has already said. Free text. This is how disputes and "the city wants a survey" show up.

Do not ask the visitor to decide whether a record of survey is required. That is the licensed surveyor's call after research.

The confirmation screen should say a written scope and fee come after someone looks at the property and the record, and that submitting the form is not a contract and not a scheduled field date.

## 5. Licensing and filing, for operations

This section is background for how the company operates. It is not required copy on the website for now. Do not add a license-number placeholder to the site from this brief.

The client has identified Travis as the person who will be the licensed land surveyor in responsible charge, Joe as Chief of Operations, and Christopher as operations lead. This research did not look up a license and does not contain one. Until a licensed surveyor is actually in responsible charge, the company must not offer land surveying services. Naming an intent is not a license.

Sources used: the 2026 compilation of the Professional Land Surveyors' Act published by the Board for Professional Engineers, Land Surveyors, and Geologists (https://bpelsg.ca.gov/laws/pls_act.pdf), the Board's contract-law summary (https://www.bpelsg.ca.gov/laws/contractlaw.shtml), the Board's rules PDF (https://bpelsg.ca.gov/laws/boardrules.pdf), and the matching Business and Professions Code sections on https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov. Section numbers below are Business and Professions Code unless noted.

**The Act.** Chapter 15, starting at section 8700, is the Professional Land Surveyors' Act. Section 8708 says no person shall practice land surveying unless licensed or specifically exempt, and that the titles "licensed land surveyor," "professional land surveyor," and "land surveyor" are reserved for licensees. Section 8725 says it is unlawful to practice, offer to practice, or represent oneself as a land surveyor, or to set or remove a survey monument on land one does not own, without a license or a specific exemption. Offering the service on a website is an offer. The site and the ads stay dark until a licensee is in responsible charge.

**Responsible charge.** Section 8703 defines responsible charge as independent control and direction of the observations, measurements, and descriptions. It is not financial liability. Section 8729 allows a firm to offer surveying only if a currently licensed land surveyor, or a civil engineer licensed in California before 1982, is an owner, partner, or officer in charge of the surveying practice, and all surveying is performed by or under that person's responsible charge. A person who is not licensed may be a partner or officer. That person may not be the sole owner or the sole officer of the surveying business. Section 8729(h) requires a current organization record to be filed with the Board. Joe and Christopher can have operating roles. They cannot be the responsible charge, and the firm still needs Travis, or another licensee, in the role section 8729 describes.

**Names in advertising.** Section 8729(a)(3) says that if a promotion or advertisement names an individual in the business, other than as part of the business name, it must clearly state that person's license discipline. If the site later names Travis, it has to identify the license. If it names Joe or Christopher, it has to be clear they are not the surveyor. Section 8751 bars using the title, or abbreviations that amount to the title, without a valid license. Section 137 allows a licensing agency to adopt a regulation requiring license numbers in advertising. The Board rule that was read, section 476(c)(6) of the Board rules, says a licensee may advertise services the license authorizes. This research did not find a Board rule that currently forces every ad to print the license number. Maps are different: section 8761 requires the licensee's name and license number on land surveying documents, and the final document must be signed and sealed.

**What the company must not say, when a public site exists later.** This is operational guidance for that future site, not a block of text to paste now.

- Do not promise a guaranteed line, a guaranteed city approval, or a guaranteed insurance outcome. Section 8770.6 says "certify" or "certification" on a survey document is a professional opinion, not a warranty or guarantee.
- Do not give legal advice. Boundary disputes, adverse possession, and whether an SB 9 application qualifies are legal and planning questions. Geometrix already tells clients to hire an attorney for condo CC&Rs. Board rule 476(a) says a licensee may rely on other professionals, including attorneys, for the meaning of the law. The form can collect facts. It should not tell the visitor who owns the fence.
- Do not promise a fixed field completion date before someone has looked at the record and the site. Whether a record of survey must be filed depends on what the field survey discloses (section 8762). County examination has its own clock (section 8766: 20 working days, unless the parties agree to more). A "same-day proposal," which Geometrix advertises, is a different claim from a finished survey.
- Do not use the surveyor title, or say the company is licensed, before a licensee is in responsible charge. Sections 8708, 8725, and 8751.
- Board rule 476(c)(1) and (c)(4) prohibit misrepresenting qualifications. Rule 476(c)(7) limits professional opinions to what has a basis in fact, experience, or accepted practice.

**Written contract.** Section 8759 requires a written contract before work starts, unless the client knowingly agrees in writing that work may start first, or another exemption applies. The Board's contract page lists the same items. The contract has to include the services, the basis of compensation and the payment method, the name, address, and license number of the surveyor and the client's name and address, how extra services will be handled, how either party can terminate, and whether the person in responsible charge has professional liability insurance. "Written" includes an electronic contract. Exemptions include work the client will not pay for, a client who already paid under a prior contract, a knowing written waiver, and services rendered to other licensed design professionals, contractors, or a public agency. A homeowner who submits the web form is not in those exemptions. The form is a request. The contract is a later step, signed by the licensee.

**Record of survey.** Section 8762(a) says the surveyor may file a record of survey. Section 8762(b) says the surveyor shall file one when the field survey discloses any of the listed conditions, including material evidence that is not on an earlier map, a material discrepancy with a recorded map, evidence that could support a materially different line, points or lines whose positions cannot be determined from the recorded map, or points set for a deed description that are not on a recorded map. Section 8762(c) says that required map is filed within 90 days after monuments are set or the field survey is complete, whichever is first, with a delay process in subdivision (d). Section 8765 lists cases that do not need a record of survey, including a retracement that finds no material discrepancy and enough monuments, provided a corner record is filed when corners are set, reset, or found to be of a different character. The quote has to be able to say "filing not determined yet."

**Corner record and monuments.** Section 8773 requires a corner record for public-land survey corners (with a record of survey instead after a lost corner is reestablished). A surveyor may also file a corner record for property corners. Section 8771 requires monuments that control subdivisions, roads, or survey control to be referenced before they are paved over or otherwise destroyed, and a corner record or record of survey filed. Contour's site already markets this to contractors. Section 8772 requires monuments a surveyor sets on a property line to be tagged with the license number.

**Right of entry.** Section 8774 gives licensees a right of entry to look for boundary evidence. The owner or tenant must provide reasonable access. Notice is required where practicable. The intake question about gates, dogs, and tenants is how the firm gives that notice. It is not a substitute for it.

Nothing in this section authorizes the website, an ad, or a sales email to offer surveying before the license and the organization record are in place.
