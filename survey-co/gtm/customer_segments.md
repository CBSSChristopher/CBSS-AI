# [Survey Co] customer segments

Prepared November 10, 2026. [Survey Co] is a new land surveying firm for the San Francisco Bay Area. Habu, a real estate report company, holds a stake and is a referral source. This file ranks who buys surveys, what they buy, and why demand exists here. It is written so the folder can move to its own repository as-is.

Figures below are copied from the cited source. Where a number is a planning judgment rather than a published statistic, it is marked **Estimate**. No price in this file is a [Survey Co] quote.

## Service area

Nine counties, and nothing past Gilroy:

- San Francisco
- San Mateo
- Santa Clara, through Gilroy (Morgan Hill and Gilroy are in; Hollister and the rest of San Benito County are out)
- Alameda
- Contra Costa
- Marin
- Sonoma
- Napa
- Solano

That is the nine-county Association of Bay Area Governments region. Santa Cruz, Monterey, San Joaquin, and Sacramento are out even when a parcel is close to the line. Gilroy is the southern edge, not a starting point for work farther south.

## How the ranking works

Two separate ratings, then a launch order.

**Volume** is how many buyers in this territory can realistically send work in a year. **Value** is how large and how repeatable a typical assignment is. Value uses published starting prices from one Northern California firm as an external illustration only. Those are not market averages and not [Survey Co] fees.

**Launch rank** is the order for the first 90 days given the evidence plus one operating constraint: one licensed land surveyor (Travis) does field work only, and Christopher and Joe run sales, scheduling, and client communication. A segment can be valuable and still sit lower if a new one-crew firm cannot win or deliver it yet.

| Launch rank | Segment | Volume | Value per job | Why this order |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Architects and engineers | Medium | Medium-high, and it repeats | They specify the surveyor on the next project. Topographic and boundary work fits one crew. |
| 2 | Homeowners (fence, ADU, SB 9) | High | Low on a simple fence; medium when a Record of Survey or lot split is required | ADU permits are the demand series that is actually counted. Search and Google Business Profile can reach them without a sales team. |
| 3 | Real estate agents and brokers | High as a channel | The fee is the homeowner or builder job they send | California residential escrows usually do not order a survey. Agents still hit boundary, fence, addition, and lot-split questions on listings. |
| 4 | Title and escrow companies | Low frequency, high trust | High when the product is an ALTA/NSPS survey | Insurers do not pay for the survey unless they agree in writing. The order comes when someone wants survey matters off the policy, or on a commercial deal. |
| 5 | Builders and developers | Medium | Highest (parcel maps, staking, ALTAs) | Right clients, slower to earn, and easy to overload a single field crew. |
| 6 | Attorneys (boundary disputes) | Low | High, and often urgent | Good work. Take it when the field calendar can absorb a deadline. |
| 7 | Lenders | Low as a direct buyer | High, and almost always an ALTA | Reach them through the title officer and the borrower. A separate lender campaign is duplicate effort. |
| 8 | HOAs and property managers | Low-medium | Low-medium | Fences, encroachments, and common-area lines. Steady only after a few managers know the firm. |
| 9 | Cities and utilities | Low count of buyers | High per contract | Bids, insurance limits, and public-works rules. Joe can watch for later. Not a first-90-day motion. |

### Housing demand that creates surveys

HCD required the Bay Area to plan for **441,176** housing units in the 2023–2031 RHNA cycle (114,442 very-low, 65,892 low, 72,712 moderate, 188,130 above-moderate). HCD approved ABAG’s plan on January 12, 2022. This is a planning obligation, not a permit count and not a survey count. Most of those units will not each generate a separate residential boundary survey.

Sources: ABAG RHNA page, `https://abag.ca.gov/our-work/housing/rhna-regional-housing-needs-allocation`; HCD approval letter, January 12, 2022.

ADU permits are the counted homeowner flow. They are not the same thing as “surveys ordered.” Many ADU submittals use a site plan that is not a Record of Survey. The practical opening is the architect, designer, or homeowner who needs lines, easements, and grades before they commit to a building location.

Two series disagree by enough that they should not be added together:

- The City of San José’s own blog (May 30, 2025) says the city issued **10** ADU building permits in 2015 and **488** in 2024. As of March 2025 it had received nearly **60** SB 9 subdivision filings and over two dozen SB 9 single-family and duplex filings, plus **two** ADU condominium map filings after it allowed ADUs to be sold as separate property in 2024. The same post says a legally built house in San José may be eligible for up to three ADUs. Source: `https://www.sanjoseca.gov/Home/Components/News/News/6693/4765`.
- HyreADU’s calculation from HCD Annual Progress Report Table A2 (file updated September 4, 2026; extract taken September 5, 2026; permit units deduplicated by jurisdiction and project id) reports 2024 and 2025 permit units of **558 and 502** for San José, **230 and 171** for San Francisco, and **213 and 192** for Oakland. Source: `https://hyreadu.com/statistics/california-adu/`.

Use the city’s 488 when repeating San José’s claim. Use HyreADU when comparing those three cities on one method. The gap (488 versus 558 for San José in 2024) is why this plan does not publish a nine-county ADU total. Pull that total later from the HCD dashboard after someone checks the download.

Menlo Park, as reported by The Almanac from city staff (March 7, 2025): **68** ADU permits issued in 2024, **131** ADUs permitted over two years against a housing-element projection of 85 for 2023–2031, and **176** new dwelling units permitted in 2024. Source: `https://www.almanacnews.com/news/2025/03/07/progress-report-shows-menlo-park-is-on-track-with-housing-construction/`.

SB 9 (the HOME Act) has produced far fewer units than the ADU laws. The Terner Center’s count for 13 cities through November 2022, published January 18, 2023, includes these Bay Area cities:

| City | Lot-split applications | Lot splits approved | SB 9 unit applications | Unit applications approved |

| Saratoga | 21 | 2 | 15 | 1 |

Source: Terner Center, “California’s HOME Act Turns One,” `https://ternercenter.berkeley.edu/research-and-policy/sb-9-turns-one-applications/`.

A later statewide reading of annual progress reports, published by Recording Law, says cities reported 18,461 ADU units approved in 2024 against 977 SB 9 units, and 17,224 ADUs against 532 SB 9 units in 2025. That is a secondary compilation, not an HCD press total, and it is statewide. ### What a survey can cost, as published by one firm

Confluence Land Surveying, a Northern California firm, publishes these starting points and says they are approximations, not quotes. They are included so “value” has a public anchor. They are not [Survey Co] prices.

- Consulting, research, and non-field work: time and materials, $1,000 minimum
- Corner records (simple boundary): start around $2,000
- Monument preservation corner records: start around $1,500
- Records of Survey: start around $5,000
- Topographic mapping: starts around $3,000
- Lot line adjustments: start around $7,500
- Parcel maps: start around $10,000
- ALTA/NSPS in Northern California, as that firm describes it: generally $2,000 to $15,000 or more

Source: `https://confluencesurvey.com/faqs`, retrieved November 10, 2026.

County fees are a separate pass-through. Santa Clara County’s surveyor fee schedule for FY 2026–2027 lists a Record of Survey review minimum of **$1,299** (billable, 3% information-technology fee applies), a corner-record submittal and filing fee of **$15**, and a surveyor’s office hourly rate of **$345**. A companion county PDF is labeled as proposed pending Board adoption. Confirm the adopted schedule at the county fee page before putting $1,299 in a client quote. Sources: `https://stgenpln.blob.core.windows.net/document/Fees_Surveyor.pdf` and `https://plandev.santaclaracounty.gov/fees`. Other counties set their own fees. Do not reuse the Santa Clara number outside Santa Clara County.

### License and product facts that shape the offer

- Property-line work is practice under the Professional Land Surveyors’ Act. Travis is the licensed land surveyor and stays in responsible charge of the technical work and the stamp. Sales and client contact are not part of that.
- A Record of Survey is mandatory, not optional, when a field survey of boundaries discloses any of the conditions in Business and Professions Code section 8762(b), including a material discrepancy, evidence of an alternate line position, or points set that are not already on a recorded map. It is due within 90 days of setting monuments or of finishing the field survey, whichever is first (section 8762(c)).
- ALTA/NSPS Land Title Surveys ordered on or after February 23, 2026 use the 2026 Minimum Standard Detail Requirements. The 2021 standards are superseded. The standards say the title insurer is not responsible for the survey cost unless the insurer authorizes that in writing. Sources: ALTA/NSPS 2026 standards, `https://www.alta.org/policies-and-standards/policy-forms/download?formID=721&type=pdf`; NSPS summary, `https://nsps.us.com/page/2026ALTA/`.
- The statutory Transfer Disclosure Statement (Civil Code section 1102.6) asks sellers about unpermitted alterations, code compliance, fill and soil, and similar conditions. Broker questionnaires used in Bay Area transactions also ask about boundaries, encroachments, and prior surveys. This plan does not quote those forms. The trigger is an agent or seller who cannot say where the line is.

## Segment 1. Architects and engineers

**Volume: medium. Value: medium-high and repeat. Launch rank: 1.**

**Pain points.** A designer cannot place an ADU, addition, or new house on a Bay Area lot until someone has located the boundaries, easements, and grades. Hills, odd lots, and old maps make “the assessor sketch looks fine” a real risk. They lose weeks when a surveyor will not answer or will not state what the deliverable is (CAD, PDF, datum, contours, tree locations, utility evidence).

**Triggers.** A new design contract. A planning pre-application. A site that fails a setback check. A client who just learned an ADU or an SB 9 split might be allowed.

**Survey types they buy.** Topographic / existing-conditions survey, boundary survey, and often both on one map. Sometimes a FEMA Elevation Certificate if the site is in a mapped floodplain. Construction staking comes later, usually through the builder, but the architect who liked the design survey is the introduction.

**How to reach them.** Direct outreach by Christopher or Joe to residential architects, ADU designers, and small civil firms. A one-page sample deliverable (with the client’s permission, and with the address removed). LinkedIn. Local AIA and small-firm studios, not a purchased email blast. Ask each firm what file format and layer names they want, then keep that as a note on the partner record.

**Bay Area demand.** ADU permit volume in San José, San Francisco, and Oakland is large enough to keep residential designers busy (figures in the market section). Pre-approved ADU plan programs, including Menlo Park’s and the Santa Clara County collaborative at `https://aduscc.org/` (cited in Campbell’s housing-element program report), still need a lot-specific survey. **Reasoning, not a city rule:** a pre-approved plan does not locate that owner’s property corners. Whether the building department requires a licensed survey depends on that city’s checklist. Confirm the checklist. Do not tell a homeowner a survey is legally required in every city.

## Segment 2. Homeowners (fences, ADUs, SB 9)

**Volume: high. Value: low to medium. Launch rank: 2.**

**Pain points.** They do not know whether they need a corner record, a full boundary retracement, or a Record of Survey. They are comparing a neighbor’s old map, a fence bid, and a survey quote that looks high because research and county review are invisible. They are often on a permit deadline they do not control.

**Triggers.**

- A new fence, a disputed fence, or a fence bid that says “verify the line.”
- An ADU, junior ADU, addition, or garage conversion.
- An SB 9 urban lot split or two-unit project.
- A sale, refinance, or Habu report that raised a boundary or encroachment question.
- An unpermitted structure they want to legalize.

**Survey types they buy.** Fence-line or boundary retracement, corner record, Record of Survey when section 8762 requires it, topographic survey for an ADU or addition, SB 9 lot-split survey and the map the city asks for, and occasionally an Elevation Certificate.

**How to reach them.** Google Business Profile and Google Search for people already looking. The quote form on the site. Referral partners (agents, Habu, individuals). Nextdoor and Instagram for explanation, not for harvesting addresses. Do not buy Google Local Services Ads under a different trade. See the marketing plan.

**Bay Area demand.** The ADU and SB 9 figures in the market section are the evidence. San José’s May 2025 blog is the clearest local statement: hundreds of ADU permits a year, a growing SB 9 filing count, and a new (still small) path to sell an ADU as its own property, which the city described in that post. SB 9 lot splits require the owner-occupancy affidavit the ADU laws do not. That is one reason researchers give for ADUs outrunning SB 9. Both products can still need a survey. Recording Law summarizes those legal differences and notes that a 2026 bill to loosen the SB 9 owner-occupancy rule had not become law as of its August 31, 2026 account (`https://www.recordinglaw.com/data-stories/california-adu-permits-vs-sb9/`).

**Estimate, labeled.** If even a modest share of ADU applicants in the three largest cities ask for a boundary or topo survey, the raw permit counts (hundreds per city per year) are enough to matter to a one-crew firm. This plan does not convert permits into a lead forecast. There is no sourced survey-to-permit ratio.

## Segment 3. Real estate agents and brokers

**Volume: high as a channel. Value: the job they refer. Launch rank: 3.**

**Pain points.** A listing goes live with a fence that may not be on the line, an addition that may cross a setback, or a buyer who wants to know if an ADU or a lot split is even dimensionally possible. The agent needs a plain answer and a surveyor who will not embarrass them with the client. They also need to know the firm will respond, because the listing calendar will not wait.

**Triggers.** Taking a listing, writing an offer, a seller questionnaire they cannot complete, a buyer asking “can we build,” a Habu or inspection report that flags an encroachment, or a lot-line question in disclosures.

**Survey types they buy.** They usually do not buy in their own name. They introduce the owner for a boundary or fence survey, an ADU feasibility survey, an SB 9 exhibit, or, less often, a survey to address a title exception. Commercial agents are different: they are in the ALTA conversation with the lender and the title officer.

**How to reach them.** One partner page and referral form (see the sales playbook). Introductions through Habu. Office meetings at brokerages, requested by Christopher or Joe, not cold calls to every licensee. A short “what to say when a seller asks if the fence is on the line” card.

The California Department of Real Estate’s May 2026 comparative statistics report 117,066 current brokers and 292,264 current salespersons statewide (409,330 total). Source: DRE RE 149, May 2026, `https://dre.ca.gov/files/pdf/stats/RE149_2026_05.pdf`. DRE does not break that report out by county. This plan does not invent a Bay Area agent count. Build the contact list from brokerages the firm actually wants, plus Habu’s introductions.

**Bay Area demand.** High property values make a fence or a setback expensive to guess at. ADU and SB 9 questions show up in listing conversations, which is why San José planners briefed Silicon Valley agents in May 2025 (city blog cited above). The agent channel is how [Survey Co] hears about those questions before the owner starts Googling.

## Segment 4. Title and escrow companies

**Volume: low frequency. Value: high when the product is an ALTA. Launch rank: 4.**

**Pain points.** A title officer cannot remove a survey exception without a survey the insurer will accept. Commercial files stall while the parties argue about Table A items. Residential files in California usually close with the survey exception still on the policy, so a title rep who is treated as if every escrow needs a survey will ignore the firm.

**Triggers.** A commercial purchase, refinance, or construction loan. A buyer or lender asking to modify or remove survey coverage. An encroachment, overlap, or access issue on the commitment. An SB 9 or parcel-map legal description that has to match the map.

**Survey types they buy.** ALTA/NSPS Land Title Survey to the 2026 standards, with the Table A items the lender and insurer actually check. Occasionally a boundary survey aimed at one exception, which is a smaller product and should be scoped that way.

**How to reach them.** Christopher or Joe visit local title officers and commercial escrow officers at the companies already active in the nine counties. Bring a one-page note on what changed in the 2026 standards (effective date, certification, Table A, and the written authorization rule) and a sample plat with the address removed. The partner form is how they send the next file. Do not ask the insurer to pay the invoice. The 2026 standards put that cost on the client unless the insurer agrees in writing.

**Bay Area demand.** Commercial property trades and construction loans in San Francisco, the Peninsula, and Silicon Valley are the ALTA pipeline. This plan does not cite a transaction count because no current, sourceable count of “deals that ordered a survey” was found. Treat title as a relationship business measured by files referred, not by how many escrows close in the county.

## Segment 5. Builders and developers

**Volume: medium. Value: highest. Launch rank: 5.**

**Pain points.** Design surveys arrive late or in the wrong CAD standard. Staking crews miss the inspection window. Parcel maps and lot-line adjustments pick up county review comments. A new firm that says yes to a tract-map deadline and then misses it will not get the next call.

**Triggers.** Land under contract, a tentative map, an SB 9 or small-lot subdivision, a custom home, an apartment or townhouse site, a lot-line adjustment between parcels they own.

**Survey types they buy.** ALTA for the acquisition, topographic design survey, parcel map or tract map support, lot-line adjustment, construction staking, as-builts, and monument preservation when the city requires it (Business and Professions Code section 8771 is the monument-preservation statute; confirm the current text before advising a client).

**How to reach them.** Through the architects and civil engineers in segment 1, then direct conversations with custom-home builders and small infill developers. Production builders and large subdivision staking wait until the crew has a second field resource. Christopher or Joe qualify the deadline before anyone promises a start date.

**Bay Area demand.** RHNA keeps cities zoning for more housing through 2031, and San José’s SB 9 filing count shows individual owners, not only large developers, are trying to split lots. Custom and infill work across the Peninsula, the East Bay hills, Marin, and the North Bay is the right first slice: shorter jobs, steeper sites, and clients who already hire architects.

## Segment 6. Attorneys for boundary disputes

**Volume: low. Value: high. Launch rank: 6.**

**Pain points.** They need a surveyor who can explain a line in a declaration without advocacy crossing into a legal opinion. They need exhibits that match the record. They need the field work done before a hearing date. They do not need a sales sequence.

**Triggers.** An encroachment, a fence fight, an adverse-possession or prescriptive-easement claim, a quiet-title action, a failed lot-line talk between neighbors, or a dispute over an easement shown on an old map.

**Survey types they buy.** Boundary survey, Record of Survey when section 8762 requires it, exhibit maps, and a declaration or deposition from the licensee in responsible charge. The licensee’s role is technical. Christopher or Joe handle scheduling and the attorney relationship. Travis does not call the client or the opposing party.

**How to reach them.** Introductions from title officers and from real estate litigators, plus a short capability note. Do not scrape court dockets and pitch the parties. If both neighbors call, stop and follow the conflict rules Christopher and Joe set with counsel before the first dispute file.

**Bay Area demand.** No reliable public count of boundary lawsuits by county was found, so none is stated. The California Good Neighbor Fence Act (Civil Code section 841) changed how neighbors allocate fence cost. It did not decide where the property line is. A cost fight and a line fight get conflated. That is the conversation, not a volume statistic.

## Segment 7. Lenders

**Volume: low as a direct buyer. Value: high. Launch rank: 7.**

**Pain points.** The loan file needs an ALTA/NSPS survey with specific Table A items, a certification the lender’s counsel accepts, and a date that matches the commitment. Residential purchase lenders in California rarely order a survey. Calling retail loan officers about fence surveys wastes both sides.

**Triggers.** Commercial acquisition, construction, or refinance. A credit union or private lender on a mixed-use or apartment property. A requirement in the commitment to provide a survey.

**Survey types they buy.** ALTA/NSPS Land Title Survey. Sometimes an Elevation Certificate for flood insurance on an improved property in a Special Flood Hazard Area. Do not assume every waterfront or creek-side parcel needs one. Order it when the lender, the insurer, or the floodplain administrator asks.

**How to reach them.** Through the title officer and the borrower’s counsel. Offer to join the call where Table A items are checked off. No separate ad campaign.

## Segment 8. HOAs and property managers

**Volume: low-medium. Value: low-medium. Launch rank: 8.**

**Pain points.** A fence, wall, or shed from an owner into common area. An unclear boundary between the association and an adjoining parcel. A capital project (drainage, paving, a slope) designed off an old tract map. Boards move slowly and want a scope they can read.

**Triggers.** A violation hearing, a reserve project, a new property-management contract, a dispute with the neighboring parcel, a city comment on an encroachment into the right of way.

**Survey types they buy.** Boundary and encroachment exhibit, topographic survey for an engineer’s improvement plans, occasional stakeout of a limit line.

**How to reach them.** Property managers who already work with Habu or with the agent partners. One board-ready sample scope. Christopher or Joe attend only when the manager asks. Do not mass-mail HOA boards.

**Bay Area demand.** The region’s stock of planned developments and hillside common areas produces this work in small pieces. No association census is cited here because no current official count was pulled.

## Segment 9. Cities and utilities

**Volume: low. Value: high per contract. Launch rank: 9.**

**Pain points.** Public-works surveys, right-of-way maps, monument preservation, and construction staking on capital projects. Vendors need insurance, a license check, and sometimes prevailing-wage compliance. A brand-new firm is a poor fit for a bid with liquidated damages.

**Triggers.** A capital project bid, an on-call surveying list, a utility relocation, a city asking for monument preservation on a paving job.

**Survey types they buy.** Control, topographic, right-of-way, staking, and as-built surveys.

**How to reach them.** Not in the first 90 days. Joe keeps a list of on-call opportunities and revisits after the firm has deliverables, insurance certificates, and a second way to staff a field day. Christopher does not promise municipal capacity he cannot schedule.

## First 90 days, in one paragraph

Spend the launch on architects and engineers, on homeowners who are already searching for a fence, ADU, or lot-split survey, and on referral partners: agents, title and escrow, Habu, and individuals. Accept small-builder work that the field calendar can hold. Let attorneys in when the date is real. Let lenders arrive with the title officer. Leave HOA boards for introductions, and leave cities and utilities for a later review by Joe.
