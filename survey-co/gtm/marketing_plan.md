# [Survey Co] marketing plan, first 90 days

Prepared November 10, 2026. This is the channel plan for a new Bay Area land surveying firm. The service area is the nine counties named in `customer_segments.md`, south only through Gilroy. Habu is an owner and a referral source. The website, quote form, and CRM are being built separately. This plan says what those tools must support. It does not invent search volumes, click prices, or lead counts.

Christopher and Joe approve anything a client or a partner can see. The AI assistant drafts, schedules reminders, and logs. Travis does field work. He is not asked to post, reply, run ads, or answer comments. If a field video includes him, that happens only with his agreement, and Christopher or Joe still publish it and answer responses.

## Google Business Profile

Do this in week 1, before spending on Search.

- Primary category: Land surveyor. Confirm the live category name in the product. A third-party tracker listed `gcid:land_surveyor` as an active Business Profile category as of November 8, 2026 (`https://gbpsandbox.com/changelog/categories/land_surveyor`). The profile UI is the authority.
- Service-area business if there is no public office. Hide a home address. Set the service area to the nine counties. Do not add San Benito, Santa Cruz, Monterey, San Joaquin, or Sacramento.
- Name, phone, and website must match the site and the ads exactly.
- Services list, in the firm’s words: boundary and fence surveys, ADU and addition surveys, SB 9 lot-split surveys, topographic surveys, ALTA/NSPS land title surveys, construction staking, elevation certificates, lot-line adjustments, parcel maps.
- Photos: crew and equipment in the field, a monument, a sample map with the address and owner removed, the licensee’s stamp area with the license number readable. No client face, house number, or license plate.
- Posts: one a week, taken from the calendar below. Christopher or Joe publish.
- Reviews: reply from Christopher or Joe within two business days. The reply thanks the person and does not restate private boundary details.
- Q&A: seed five real questions (what a boundary survey includes, how the service area works, what to send with a quote request, why a neighbor’s survey is not yours, how deposits and scheduling work). Answers must match the sales playbook.
- Messaging and the quote form both land in the CRM as a New request and alert Christopher and Joe only.

**Operating target, not a statistic:** the profile is complete, with at least eight photos and a working link to the quote form, before the first Search ad is enabled.

## Google Local Services Ads

Do not buy them in the first 90 days.

Google’s U.S. Local Services Ads page lists eligible home services. The list retrieved November 10, 2026 includes carpenter, fencing pro, general contractor, and home inspector. It does not include land surveyor. Source: `https://business.google.com/us/ad-solutions/local-service-ads/`.

Fencing is on that list. [Survey Co] does not enroll as a fencing pro, a general contractor, or any other category it is not. That would advertise the wrong license and the wrong service.

**Operating rule:** once a quarter, Christopher or Joe re-check the category list in the Local Services signup for a Bay Area ZIP. If “land surveyor” appears and the screening requirements are ones the firm can meet (license, insurance), bring a proposal back before spending. Until then the budget line stays $0. Google has also been moving some Local Services advertisers toward Performance Max pay-per-lead campaigns (Google Ads Help, supported verticals, `https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17326460`). Re-check that page at the same time. Absence from the home-services list is not a reason to force the firm into a neighboring vertical.

## Google Search Ads

### Setup

- Campaigns are Search only for the first 90 days. No Display, no Performance Max, until search-term reports are clean.
- Location: presence in the nine counties. Exclude San Benito, Santa Cruz, Monterey, San Joaquin, and Sacramento. Gilroy stays in because it is inside Santa Clara County.
- Ad schedule: weekdays 7:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. Pacific, plus Saturday 9:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m. so a weekend search is not invisible. Human response still follows the one-business-hour rule in the sales playbook. The automated receipt covers nights.
- Bidding: start on Maximize Clicks with a bid cap only after the first week shows actual auction prices. Do not type a cap from this document. There is no sourced surveyor click price here.
- Every ad points to the quote form, with the campaign name stored on the lead.
- Call reporting uses the firm number that Christopher or Joe answer. Do not send ad calls to Travis.
- Final URL and headline claims stay inside what the firm can do on day one: licensed land surveyor, nine-county service area, the products listed above. No “best,” no “cheapest,” no “#1,” no invented years in business, no star rating until reviews exist.

## Reviews

Ask after the CRM stage is Delivered/recorded, not at the proposal and not in exchange for anything.

Google’s content policy prohibits offering money, gifts, discounts, or other incentives for a review, and it prohibits review gating (sending only happy clients to the public review and unhappy clients elsewhere). Use one process for every delivered job. Source: Google contribution policy, `https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114`.

**Operating sequence:**

1. The day the deliverable is sent, Christopher or Joe email and text the client a thank-you and the direct Google review link. One ask. No second public post about that client.
2. Seven days later, if there is no review, one reminder. Then stop.
3. Separately from the review, the same note may ask whether they know an agent, architect, or neighbor who needs a survey. That referral ask is not tied to the review. Script in the sales playbook.
4. Christopher or Joe reply to every review. They do not argue a boundary in the reply. A complaint about service gets a phone call from Christopher or Joe, then a short public reply that the firm has been in touch.

Target: every delivered job gets the ask. There is no target star rating, because a target rating is how gating starts.

## Referral partnerships

The operating system (form fields, CRM tags, thank-you steps, Habu, agents, title and escrow, and individuals such as Luiza) lives in the sales playbook under “Referral partner program.” Marketing’s job is to make the path obvious.

- Footer link on every page: Partners.
- The partner page explains who it is for, what happens after a submission, and that a person at the firm replies. It does not promise a fee share.
- One downloadable one-pager for agents and one for title officers. Both end in the form URL or a QR code.
- Habu: agree the moment inside a Habu report when a boundary, fence, addition, or lot-split question should offer [Survey Co]. The handoff is the same form, with partner type Habu. Marketing does not invent a new product name for Habu.
- No paid referral bounty in the first 90 days. See the playbook for the legal reason. Thank-you, status, and a fast response are the offer.
- Track partner-sourced leads with the CRM source field. Do not judge the channel by likes.

**Estimate of outreach capacity, not a pipeline forecast:** Christopher or Joe together hold 15 new partner conversations across the 90 days (architects, agents, title, or individuals). That number is a workload cap so partner outreach does not displace the one-hour response rule. It is not an expected close count.

## Email nurture

Three sequences. The sales cadence in the playbook wins whenever a person is in Quote sent or a later open stage. Marketing email stops for that person until the job is Delivered/recorded or Closed lost.

From address: a named mailbox Christopher or Joe actually read (for example hello@[survey-co domain]). The AI assistant may draft and queue. Sending to a client or partner is approved by Christopher or Joe in the first 90 days, until they explicitly turn on a template.

### A. Homeowner who asked a question and is not in an active quote

Day 0. Subject: We received your note about [city]. Body: what was received, the one-business-hour reply promise, and the three facts that change a scope (address, what they want to build or fence, and any deadline). This is the automated receipt. It does not count as the human reply.

Day 3, only if no human quote conversation is open. Subject: Fence line, ADU, or lot split? Body: three short paragraphs on which product is which, link to pages 2, 3, and 4. No price.

Day 10. Subject: What we need to tell you if a survey is even the right tool. Body: sometimes the recorded map already answers the question; sometimes section 8762 means a Record of Survey has to be filed. Invite them to send the address.

Day 21. Subject: Still thinking about the property in [city]? Body: one paragraph, link to the quote form, and a line that says we will not keep emailing if they reply “stop.”

### B. Partners (agents, title, Habu colleagues, individuals)

Triggered when Christopher or Joe add someone as a partner, not when a stranger downloads a PDF.

Week 0. Subject: How to send [Survey Co] a property. Body: form link, what we do on receipt, and the promise that Christopher or Joe reply to the client.

Week 3. Subject: What your client can expect on a fence or an ADU survey. Body: scope in plain language, deposit-before-field in one sentence, service area.

Week 7. Subject: A note for title files, if that is your work. Body: 2026 ALTA standards, Table A has to be chosen, the insurer does not pay the survey unless they agree in writing. Agents can ignore this one; suppress by partner type if the CRM allows.

Week 12. Subject: Thank you for keeping us in mind. Body: no offer, no gift card. A reminder of the form and a request to tell us if a referral was clumsy so we can fix it.

### C. After delivery

Day 0. Subject: Your survey for [street name only]. Body: download or transmittal link, what was filed if something was filed, the Google review link, and the referral sentence. Review policy above.

Day 7. Subject: If the map raised a question. Body: they can reply to this email and reach Christopher or Joe. One review reminder if they have not left one. Then this sequence ends.

Suppression: anyone in Closed lost for “do not contact,” any hard bounce, any unsubscribe. Honor it on the first request.

## What to measure

Read these every Monday. Christopher or Joe. Thirty minutes.

| Measure | Source | How to read it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New requests by campaign, by partner, by city | CRM | Which channel produced real addresses inside the service area |
| Human response within one business hour | CRM timestamps | A miss is a process problem, not a copy problem |
| Share of leads outside the nine counties | CRM | If this is common, fix locations and negatives before adding budget |
| Search terms that spent money | Google Ads | Add negatives the same day |
| Quote sent and Deposit paid, by source | CRM | The only quality signal that matters in 90 days |
| Profile views, calls, and direction requests | Google Business Profile | Direction requests should stay low if the address is hidden |
| Review asks sent versus reviews received | CRM task | Completeness of the ask, not a star target |
| Pages published | Site | Count against the 20, with a named reviewer |

Do not report cost per lead until at least 30 days of Search spend and a CRM source on every lead. A partial month will lie.
